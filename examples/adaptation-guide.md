# Adaptation Guide: Applying This Skill to Different Problem Domains

The Team Dua style transfers cleanly to any service-design problem as long as the structural patterns are preserved. Below are three sketch adaptations showing how the 4-phase system applies to different domains.

---

## Example A: Elderly Loneliness in Urban India

### Phase 1 funnel
- Global: Loneliness as a public health crisis worldwide
- National: India — joint families eroding, 23 million elderly live alone
- Sub-group: Elderly widows in metros
- Individual: A gerontologist who is also a daughter of an elderly parent

### Phase 2 expansion
- Stakeholder map: Adult children, neighbors, community health workers, temple volunteers, municipal services, geriatric doctors
- Personas: Retired professor (high resource, digital literate) vs. Former domestic worker (low resource, no pension)
- Problem space: Social isolation, medical access, daily routines, mobility, financial insecurity, cultural invisibility

### Phase 3 convergence
- Chosen problem: Daily companionship gap
- Rejected: Medical access (requires healthcare specialization), Financial insecurity (structural policy issue)
- Sub-HMWs: emotional connection / routine-building / intergenerational bridging

### Phase 4 solution sketch
- A community-based daily check-in and companionship program embedded in housing societies
- Features: Volunteer matching, daily structured activities, emergency alert, family dashboard

## Example B: Student Mental Health in Tier-2 Indian Cities

### Phase 1 funnel
- Global: Rising student anxiety and depression rates
- National: India's academic pressure culture + limited counseling infrastructure
- Sub-group: First-generation college students in tier-2 cities
- Individual: A professor who is also a first-generation graduate

### Phase 2 expansion
- Stakeholders: Parents, hostel wardens, college counselors, peer groups, coaching center operators, local mental health NGOs
- Personas: Engineering student from agricultural family vs. Commerce student from small-business family
- Problem space: Academic pressure, financial stress, social isolation, language barriers, career anxiety, family expectations

### Phase 3 convergence
- Chosen problem: Peer-support access gap
- Sub-HMWs: normalizing help-seeking / creating accessible peer support / bridging language/culture barriers

### Phase 4 solution sketch
- Peer-led mental wellness circles within college hostels with trained student facilitators
- Features: Facilitator training program, anonymous check-in system, escalation protocol, faculty liaison

## Example C: Gig Worker Financial Wellbeing

### Phase 1 funnel
- Global: Gig economy growth + financial precarity
- National: India's 7.7 million gig workers (NITI Aayog) without benefits
- Sub-group: Delivery drivers in the first 2 years of gig work
- Individual: A financial literacy educator who also drives for a platform

### Phase 2 expansion
- Stakeholders: Platform companies, local kirana shops (credit networks), families, insurance agents, government labor departments
- Personas: Full-time delivery driver supporting parents vs. Part-time driver supplementing a day job
- Problem space: Income volatility, no emergency fund, health insurance gaps, digital financial literacy, social stigma, physical safety

### Phase 3 convergence
- Chosen problem: Income smoothing gap
- Sub-HMWs: predictable savings / emergency access / financial literacy in context

### Phase 4 solution sketch
- Auto-savings mechanism integrated with platform payouts + micro-insurance bundling
- Features: Smart split (auto-save % of each payout), emergency fund with instant access, insurance enrollment via familiar interfaces

## The Transfer Test

For any new domain, verify these structural elements transfer:

| Element | Must be present | Adapted how |
|---------|----------------|-------------|
| Funnel (Phase 1) | Global > National > Sub-group > Individual | Change the noun phrases, keep the narrowing |
| Emotional arc (Phase 1) | 5 stages, zigzag trajectory | Derive stages from your user's life timeline |
| Contrasting personas (Phase 2) | 2 personas on a meaningful contrast axis | Pick the axis that reveals the most about your problem |
| Problem scoring (Phase 3) | Team members score individually on criteria | Keep the individual-first, discuss-second protocol |
| Need statement (Phase 3) | User + Need (qualities) + Insight (outcome) | Never name a solution form in the need statement |
| Solution name (Phase 4) | Name with built-in meaning | Workshop with regional language, mythology, or action words |
| Service flow (Phase 4) | 9-12 steps ending on systemic outcome | Last step is always the emergent societal benefit |
